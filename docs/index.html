This page ships the pixel field of the adopted, format-validated artifact — measured by scripts/build_submission_payload.py as an gems-rle-v1 stream of 141,156 runs for 12,279,160 pixels. Click Build submission.tif below: your browser decodes it, writes the GeoTIFF locally with docs/geotiff_writer.js, re-reads its own bytes against the pinned pixels, and offers the download only when every self-check passes. No install, no GPU, no download of the 418 MB feature stack, and nothing is uploaded — the file appears in your Downloads folder, under a name unique to that build (gems-submission-<UTC instant>-<artifact sha8>.tif), with a suggested short Note to paste into the dialog beside it — the comment that tells your team's submissions apart later. Consequence, stated plainly. The training labels in the data tab are the existing USGS Quaternary compilation, but both prize phases score the expert-mapped new fault dataset. A submission that reproduces the catalogue earns credit only where the experts' new labels coincide with it, and every DTI printed elsewhere on this site is a monitor against the wrong population. That asymmetry is also why the emission width was measured rather than assumed: the adopted policy is a thinned skeleton (width 0 px) at floor 0.1 — at that floor the measured width curve peaks at 0 px on every ensemble, so a wider band would lower the score, not raise it — see the width measurement.

GEMSDOE4 — the new-fault-first line

The repository's earlier lines selected their emission policy on the catalogue population. The rules score the new faults in both prize phases (quoted verbatim above and on Metric), so GEMSDOE4 is a different strategy rather than another variant of the same model:

  1. Lineament features instead of raw bands. Multi-scale Sato ridgeness, structure-tensor coherence and windowed context on the 6 bands that carry an edge signal (src/lineament_features.py, 63 features), fed to a HistGradientBoostingClassifier. A tree model can split on "this pixel sits on a 2 km line"; it cannot invent that from 19 uncorrelated band values.
  2. Supervision from an independent fault compilation. The catalogue teaches the model the faults geologists already mapped — the ones with the most obvious geophysical expression. Adding the SGMC-derived proxy labels (data/evidence/proxy/proxy_catalogue.tif) gives it examples of faults the catalogue does not contain, which is the population the metric rewards. The catalogue-only ablation is a measured number, not an assumption (--no-proxy-labels).
  3. Selection on the new-fault population, on geography the model never saw. Fold 0 selects the emission rule by the proxy DTI over its own blocks; fold 1 measures it and is never scored by the sweep. The catalogue DTI is reported next to it, never used to select.
  4. A union of structurally different detectors. Coverage is cheap under this metric (FN carries β = 0.8, FP only α = 0.2), so detectors whose errors differ are worth more together than apart: the 11-fold deep ensemble, the classical raw-band GBM, and three lineament NFF detectors - two at different seeds on the same population and one supervised on the independent compilation's own pixels only (--supervision proxy_only), whose errors are structurally anti-correlated with every catalogue-trained member.

Why the whole grid is not a substitute. The whole grid mixes geography that every member has seen with geography that none has: the members were run with different folds (--fold 0 --eval-fold 1 for some, 2/3 for others), so a whole-grid score is partly a measurement of memorisation no matter which member you look at. Scoring every candidate on the selection fold at least matches the two folds the measured members held out - and the next block is the audit of exactly that claim, per member, from each member's own report. Fixing the scope changed the shipped rule: the k = 2 family was the only one the old --votes default could express, and k = 3 measures better on both the selection fold and (in the same run) the fold the sweep never scored.

mean over treated members of [score on the union's folds - score on the rest] minus the same mean over controls, halved. Halving is only exact if the in-sample boost is the same kind of boost on both fold sets (B_01 = B_23); the two groups are built so the fold-difficulty term cancels exactly, and the residual is exposure - with that symmetry assumption as its only gap, stated here rather than hidden. A harsh COMMON floor inflates the magnitude: the honest side of the contrast can be near zero Treated members average 0.0138, controls -0.0948, so the in-sample exposure is 0.0543. A policy selected on a scope that is in-sample for part of the member pool is selected on a mixture the sweep cannot claim. The clean pool is the set of members whose holdout IS the union's scope; see data/evidence/combined_clean/report.json.

Caveat, stated here rather than left to the reader. In-sample-ness does not make a member a bad detector - the private scored set is a different population of faults, not different geography. It makes the MEASUREMENT optimistic, and therefore any policy chosen with it.

The emission budget: what a higher score actually costs

The official metric collapses to a two-term identity, verified numerically against src.metrics.GtContext on every scope below (max relative error 2.1e-16):

DTI = TP_w / (alpha*(TP_w + FP_w) + beta*|G| + eps)

the prediction enters only through the weighted coverage TP_w and the weighted false-positive mass FP_w; one unit of coverage is worth beta/alpha = 4 units of false-positive mass - with β/α = 4.0, one more unit of coverage is worth four units of false-positive mass. The prediction enters through exactly two numbers, so this is the whole design space:

The control arm: the same search with no in-sample members at all

The audit above says the shipped pool contains two members that trained on the folds it selects and measures on. That cannot be fixed by re-scoring - it is fixed by re-running those members (queued), or by excluding them and repeating the whole search. The second is what this arm does: four members (classical, nff42, nff44, po46), every one of which held out both union folds, 200 candidates, the same pre-registered grid. The task

Predict where geological faults are in the GeoDAWN region of northwestern Nevada — faults being surface indicators of geothermal resources. The submission is a single-band float32 GeoTIFF of per-pixel probabilities on the competition grid (submission format).

The twist that shapes everything: the public USGS fault database is incomplete. Scoring uses a private set of newly-expert-labelled faults absent from that database, and after the initial round an expert panel reviews every team's predictions to expand the label set for a final re-scoring (competition structure). Predicting the known faults back is therefore not the goal; finding the unmapped ones is.

Key findings so far

  1. The feature stack has 19 bands, not the 10 implied by the problem page's prose. All 19 carry band_name/data_category tags. Full list on the Data page.
  2. example_submission.tif is not "total fault absence". It is bit-identical to the label raster (60,988 positive pixels in both). The problem page describes it as predicting total absence. Flagged as an irregularity — see Data.
  3. The metric rewards precision, not generosity. Despite β/α = 4 nominally punishing misses, predicting everything scores 0.096 while predicting a precise quarter of the faults scores 0.554. See Metric for the algebra and the measured table.
  4. The DEM link PDF has no text layer. It is a scan; three text extractors return zero URLs. Recovered by OCR and then re-confirmed tile-by-tile against the live USGS bucket.
  5. A whole ensemble run was reported green while producing no submission at all. The blend crashed on a TIFF tile-size rule, the crash was masked by a missing pipefail, and a 110-byte stub was committed as submission.tif. Found by auditing the committed artifacts on 2026-09-16 and fixed; see Results.
  6. Both prize phases score new faults only. Verbatim from the official rules (§1.1, §3.3, §3.5): the training labels are the existing catalogue, the labelled test data are faults missing from it. The catalogue DTI quoted everywhere is a plumbing monitor, not a leaderboard proxy — Metric quotes the sentences.

The bar, from the competition itself: the best public leaderboard score is 0.2854 DW-Tversky (50 ranked entrants, median 0.1134), read from the leaderboard at 2026-09-21T04:09:25+00:00. This repository is not on it yet — see Verification.

Where this stands

2026-09-17 (session 10): the ensemble that was supposed to answer the width question had already lost six fully-trained folds. Run 35170395055 — the queued second ensemble, fired in session 9 — trained all six folds for ~3 hours and then staged nothing: the workflow told the trainer mc_id = matrix.fold (0…5) while the staging step looked for heldout_mc(FOLD_OFFSET + fold).npz (6…11), so cp failed on every fold, the artifact upload was skipped, and the blend job correctly refused an incomplete fold set. The failure record could not be committed either, because the blend job's Checkout had been skipped and the evidence step ran without a .git directory. The fold identity now reaches the trainer, staging globs whatever the trainer wrote and asserts the manifest's fold id, uploads run even when staging fails, and tests/test_ensemble_workflow.py executes the workflow's own shell against planted artefacts (re-introducing either defect fails 4 tests). The second ensemble has been re-fired on the fixed workflow.

The width question got a direct measurement, and the answer is uncomfortable. Instead of sweeping floors on the probability map, scripts/measure_miss_distance.py takes the shipped submission, grows its emitted pixels by k and scores with the official metric against the USGS faults the training labels do not contain. The median unseen-fault pixel sits 22 px = 2.2 km from anything the model emitted, only 4.9% of that truth is inside the metric's own 300 m tolerance, and widening the band to 16 px lifts the score from 0.0247 to 0.0713 — the first measured policy here that beats the constant-ones baseline (0.0585). 74% of the truth has nothing emitted within 1.2 km, so the model's detection, not the band width, is the binding constraint. See Metric for the curve and the projection against the hidden scored-truth size; the default still waits for the second ensemble to reproduce the gain.
