1. Executive Overview & Problem Context

The Geologic Enhanced Mapping System (GEMS) Prize is an open innovation challenge sponsored by the U.S. Department of Energy’s (DOE) Office of Geothermal (OG), administered by the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) under the American-Made program, and hosted on the DrivenData platform.

Core Mission: Develop algorithms and machine learning models that detect and delineate geological faults indicative of geothermal resources across the high-resolution GeoDAWN survey area in northwestern Nevada and adjacent eastern California. Faults provide critical permeable pathways for hydrothermal fluids; mapping hidden and previously unmapped faults reduces exploratory drilling risk and accelerates clean energy deployment.

The Fundamental Twist (Incomplete Labels by Design): Unlike conventional machine learning competitions where participants simply maximize accuracy against known labels, the existing public USGS fault catalog is known to be incomplete. The competition's true prediction target is the discovery of new, previously unmapped faults:

  • Training Labels (data/labels.tif): Compiled from the INGENIOUS project and USGS Quaternary fault database. Positively labeled pixels represent currently known faults.
  • Test Datasets (Scored Targets): Manually delineated by expert structural geologists from the USGS and NLR, comprising newly identified faults absent from the public database.
  • Dual Evaluation: Predictions that merely reproduce the training labels do not score well on the test set. Models must generalize to real geophysical fault signatures.

6. The Scored Metric & The Winning Emission Strategy

Submissions are evaluated using the Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (DTI):

DTI(α=0.2, β=0.8) = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2 · FP_w + 0.8 · FN_w + ε)

where distances to ground truth are weighted by a linear triangular kernel with R = 300 m (3 pixels at 100m resolution):

k(d) = max(1 - d / R, 0) = max(1 - d / 300, 0)

Strategic Implications of the Metric:

  • 4:1 False Negative Asymmetry (β = 0.8, α = 0.2): Missing a real fault pixel incurs 4× the penalty of emitting a false alarm. Models must avoid excessive conservatism.
  • The In-Domain Trap: Models trained and calibrated solely on existing training labels (USGS known faults) learn a high probability threshold (t0 ≈ 0.47) and thin skeleton. On the new-fault-like proxy population, this in-domain policy collapses to a DTI of only 0.0247.
  • The Measured Winning Policy (Floor 0.1, Thin, Width 0 px): Extensive sweeps across 132 parameter combinations on multiple independent ensembles demonstrate that lowering the emission floor to 0.1 with morphological thinning dramatically lifts performance on unseen faults:
    • Ensemble 1 (Run 35042805806): DTI 0.1365 vs 0.0410 reference (+0.0954 contrast)
    • Ensemble 2 (Run 35249562910): DTI 0.0777 vs 0.0320 reference (+0.0456 contrast)
    • Shipping Field (Mean of Ensembles 1+2, Run 35275312337): DTI 0.0999 vs 0.0304 reference (+0.0695 contrast)
    • 3-Ensemble Mean (16 live folds, Run 35285326679): DTI 0.0850 vs 0.0269 reference (+0.0581 contrast)
    • Worst-Case Robustness Ranking: Ranks #1 of 132 hard candidates across all swept fields.
  • Which FIELD ships is now a pre-registered rule, not a judgment call (2026-09-19). The policy axis (floor/thin/width) was already measured and adopted; the field axis (which ensemble mean ships) was not. docs/FIELD_SELECTION_RULE.md pre-registers it: a new field may replace the shipped one only if it passes F1 (pinned re-blend provenance) and F2 (committed proxy sweep) as eligibility, then R1 (beats shipped + 0.010 margin in the primary support window), R2 (top of all three windows), R4 (beats the current policy), R5 (≥ 3 independent candidates in the window) — and R3, a paired 2,000-resample block bootstrap on both fields' raw rasters with P(new > shipped) ≥ 0.95, which is unmeasurable from committed bytes until both raw rasters are scored, so it cannot be waved through. The rule is machine-enforced: scripts/check_field_selection.py runs on every committed evidence and on every reblend.yml run before any artifact download; the committed verdict is KEEP mean12 (data/evidence/field_selection.json) and a re-blend of any other field fails the workflow until the committed evidence says ADOPT of that field with R3 measured. Seven tests in tests/test_field_selection.py pin the rule, including the refusal of a synthetic field that passes every condition except the unmeasured R3.
