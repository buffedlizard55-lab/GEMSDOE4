Method

How this differs from the reference solution, and the reasoning behind each change.

Baseline we build on

The organisers' reference solution (Prof. John Lipor) trains a U-Net (ResNet-18 encoder, ImageNet weights) on 128-px patches over 5 Monte-Carlo train/test splits with TverskyLoss(α=0.2, β=0.8), AdamW at 1×10⁻⁴, 5 epochs, and stitches patch predictions back into a full raster. Its patching creates the test patches first and zeroes them in the global image so training cannot leak test labels.

Guarding against leakage and self-deception

  • Test windows are cut and zeroed globally before overlapping training windows are sampled, and windows overlapping test area beyond a threshold are dropped.
  • The false-positive weight map is cropped from a global distance transform, so predictions near a patch border are not spuriously penalised.
  • Local scores against the public labels are reported as optimistic wherever they appear — those are the labels the model trained on, and they are not the test set.
  • src/metrics.py --self-test runs 8 checks including fast-vs-brute-force agreement and the official worked example.

The strategic problem nobody can shortcut

The scored faults are the ones missing from the public database. A model that merely fits the public labels is fitting the wrong target. Two implications drive the design:

  1. Don't over-fit the known faults. Held-out DTI against public labels is a plumbing check, not a leaderboard proxy.
  2. Generalise geophysically. The useful signal is the physics that produces a fault expression — magnetic/gravity gradient discontinuities, strain-rate concentration, detrended-elevation lineaments — which is why all 19 bands are kept and line-enhancement filtering is available in post-processing. The Final Prize Round explicitly rewards flagging faults experts then confirm.
